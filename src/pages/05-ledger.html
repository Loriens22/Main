The intervention ledger

What survives contact with a well-designed experiment. The honest answer is: a short list, mostly sex-specific, mostly in mice — and a very long list of things that do not work at all.

The one dataset that deserves your trust

The NIA Interventions Testing Program is the closest thing geroscience has to a clean instrument. It runs every compound at three independent sites, in genetically heterogeneous UM-HET3 four-way-cross mice — not an inbred strain with its own private pathologies — with both sexes analysed separately and the protocol fixed in advance. It is designed specifically to defeat the failure modes that make the rest of the mouse literature hard to read: strain-specific rescue, single-site effects, sex-pooling, and short-lived controls.

It is also the reason we know that a great many celebrated compounds do nothing.

Figure 5 · interactive NIA Interventions Testing Program — median lifespan effect by sex
Two things to notice. First, the sex-specificity is extreme and it is not noise: 17α-estradiol and canagliflozin extend male lifespan meaningfully and do nothing whatever in females; acarbose gives +22% in males and +5% in females. Any result reported sex-pooled in this field should be treated as uninterpretable until it is split. Second, switch to "including the failures" — the modal outcome of a serious, well-powered longevity experiment is exactly zero, including for several compounds sold as longevity supplements today.

The supplement graveyard

Every one of these was tested by the ITP in genetically heterogeneous mice at three sites, and every one produced no lifespan extension: resveratrol, curcumin, green tea extract, fish oil, medium-chain triglyceride oil, oxaloacetate, α-ketoglutarate, ursodeoxycholic acid, nicotinamide riboside, and fisetin — the last two being among the most heavily marketed longevity compounds in the world. Metformin failed alone, though it did not block rapamycin's effect when combined.

This is what a properly powered negative result looks like, and it is the most valuable output the field produces. It is also the least cited.

What this looks like in humans

The translation is brutal. The interventions with the largest, best-established human all-cause mortality effects are not the ones the longevity industry sells.

Read this table through the framework

Every entry above with real evidence is a hazard multiplier — an offset. Semaglutide's HR of 0.81 corresponds to k = 1.23, which by the Logarithmic Trap is worth ln(1.23)/0.0866 ≈ 2.4 years if sustained lifelong at that magnitude. That is a genuinely valuable result and it is also, precisely, the ceiling of that class of intervention. Nothing in this table is a rate change, and nothing in it was ever tested for being one.
