Practical implications

What actually works today

The framework is pessimistic about ceilings and optimistic about direction. Neither changes what an individual should do this year, which is unglamorous and well established.

Not medical advice

This section describes what the evidence base shows at population level. It is not individualised advice, and several interventions discussed elsewhere in this document have serious documented harms. Nothing here should be used to start, stop or modify treatment.

The honest summary of the evidence in §5 is that the interventions with the best-established human mortality effects are the ones nobody can sell you. They are also, without exception, offsets — which means they are bounded, and it means they work.

Each bar converts a reported hazard ratio into years of age-offset via Δ = ln(1/HR)/α. These are illustrative order-of-magnitude conversions, not predictions for an individual — they assume the hazard ratio is causal, applies at all ages, and is sustained lifelong, none of which is exactly true, and the observational entries are certainly overstated for that reason. The summary in one paragraph

Do not smoke. Be physically fit — cardiorespiratory fitness is the strongest measured correlate of survival in the entire literature, even after discounting heavily for the healthy-user bias that inflates it. Keep blood pressure and lipids controlled if indicated. Sleep. Get vaccinated. Do not drink for your health — the protective J-curve is an artifact. Then stop, because the remaining evidence-based options are close to exhausted, and everything past this point is either unproven, sold to you, or both.

The entire achievable offset stack is worth perhaps 10–15 years relative to a sedentary smoker, and considerably less relative to someone already doing the basics. That is a lot of life and it is worth having. It is also, by the arithmetic of §3, close to the ceiling of what this category of intervention can ever deliver.

Epistemic status

What is wrong with this document

A study that spends fifteen sections criticising other people's methodology owes the reader an unusually complete account of its own.

Research constraints — serious, and they bound everything

  • No primary full text was read. The network policy of the environment this study was produced in blocked outbound requests to every publisher and preprint host tested — Nature, Science, Cell, PubMed, PMC, eLife, bioRxiv, arXiv, ClinicalTrials.gov, even Wikipedia — all returning HTTP 403 at the proxy. Every quantitative claim attributed to the literature comes from search-engine summaries of abstracts and secondary sources. This is a serious limitation and it is not mitigated by the number of sources consulted.
  • The search budget was exhausted mid-project. The session's 200-call limit was consumed before three of the five domain reviews had finished collecting. Several planned topics — plasma dilution, vaccination, blood-pressure and lipid evidence, dietary patterns — are consequently under-covered and are flagged as gaps rather than silently omitted.
  • Trial identifiers were not verifiable. No NCT number could be checked, so none is quoted. Trials are described rather than cited by identifier.
  • Some numbers could not be cross-checked at all and are marked as such. Where two sources disagreed, both are reported.

Weaknesses in the argument itself

  • The Gompertz model is an approximation. Real hazards decelerate at extreme ages, populations are heterogeneous in frailty, and the Makeham term does not shift. Every one of these makes the Logarithmic Trap slightly less severe than stated. None makes it go away, because the required factor is 105.
  • The resilience wall rests on an extrapolation we ourselves criticise. The published critical age comes from extending a linear fit far beyond the observed data, and we showed in §10 that two routes to it disagree by 15–40%. The wall may be a good deal softer than modelled, or may not exist as a sharp barrier at all. Figure 13 lets you turn it off for exactly this reason.
  • The assumption that the wall scales with s is a modelling choice, not a derived result. If resilience loss is driven by something that does not slow when the Gompertz slope slows, the optimistic quadrant of Figure 13 is wrong.
  • The offset/rate dichotomy is a simplification. Real interventions produce mixtures, time-varying effects, and effects that differ by cause of death. The Q–Q test handles mixtures, but the framing throughout is cleaner than biology is.
  • Rodent caloric restriction remains genuinely unresolved. If the slope-effect reading is right, a rate intervention already exists in mammals and the central claim needs revision. We report the contradiction rather than picking the side that suits the argument.
  • The naked mole-rat case is contested. Failure to detect a Gompertz slope is not proof of its absence, and the published Comments make a real statistical point about power. The existence proof is strong but not closed — and it carries more weight in this document than any single contested result comfortably should.

How this document was made

One lead model performed the synthesis, the derivations, the framework in §3–§4 and §13, and wrote the document. Five parallel agents collected domain evidence — molecular and cellular mechanisms; pharmacology and metabolism; demography and biomarkers; physics and theory; frontier engineering. The agents were instructed not to attempt synthesis, and were required to tag every number with its provenance and to prefer a null over a guess. All five terminated at their write-up stage when the account's session limit was reached; four delivered complete reports and one delivered structured data only, which is why pharmacology coverage is thinner than intended.

On AI-generated research

The reasoning, derivations and framework here are the model's own and can be checked directly — the mathematics in §3, §4 and §13 requires no trust at all, only a calculator. The literature claims are a different matter: they were assembled without reading the papers, by a system that is capable of producing fluent, plausible, and wrong citations. Treat every attributed number in this document as a lead to verify, not as a fact to cite. Where we could not verify, we said so rather than filling the gap, and the reader should hold that standard against us wherever we failed to meet it.
