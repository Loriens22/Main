What is actually built, versus what is promised

The gap between these two things is the defining feature of this sector. Sorting them requires one discipline above all: refusing to let a press release count as a result.

A note on how technology readiness is abused here. Companies routinely present the evidence of a single mouse study alongside the vocabulary of a late-stage clinical programme. Dosing one healthy volunteer in a phase 1 safety study makes you "clinical-stage" on safety while leaving you at proof-of-concept on efficacy. The chart below scores the efficacy axis — because that is the axis that determines whether anyone lives longer.

The hype ledger

A field's base rates are set by its failures, not its press releases. These are the ones that should calibrate expectations.

CaseThe claimWhat actually happened
Tracheal tissue engineering Engineered windpipes transplanted into patients; celebrated as the arrival of organ engineering. One of the most serious research-fraud scandals in modern medicine. Patients died. Papers retracted, the surgeon criminally convicted. The cautionary tale the whole regenerative field should teach and mostly does not.
Alagebrium (ALT-711) An "AGE-breaker" that would reverse arterial stiffening by cleaving crosslinks. Failed in clinical development. The category's central chemical target — glucosepane — still has no working breaker, roughly two decades on.
Unity Biotechnology (UBX0101) First senolytic into the clinic; osteoarthritis as the beachhead indication. Failed to beat placebo in phase 2. The programme was discontinued and the company nearly collapsed. The first serious clinical test of the senolytic hypothesis was negative.
resTORbio (RTB101) mTOR inhibition to restore immune function in the elderly. Failed its phase 3. This is the only adequately powered late-stage trial of an mTOR inhibitor for a geroscience-adjacent endpoint, and it did not work.
Young-plasma clinics Transfusions of young plasma sold directly to consumers as rejuvenation. The FDA issued a public safety warning against the practice. No controlled human outcome evidence supports it. The industry continues.
Telomerase self-experimentation A biotech executive self-administered telomerase gene therapy and reported reversal of biological age. Uncontrolled n = 1 self-experimentation is not evidence. No control, no blinding, no independent verification, direct commercial interest in the result.
Follistatin gene therapy abroad Gene therapy sold through jurisdictions with permissive regulation. The regulatory-arbitrage pattern: relocate to avoid the evidentiary standard rather than meet it. Products sold ahead of controlled efficacy data.
"Reversed aging by X years" headlines Recurring genre, usually built on a clock reading. Typically small, often uncontrolled, and measured on an instrument that is not a validated surrogate for anything (§8).
The base rate

Two clinical-stage geroscience companies ran adequately powered trials against hard-ish endpoints. Both failed. A decade of very well-funded industrial research has produced no approved therapy for any aging-related indication, and aging is not a recognised regulatory indication in the first place — which means there is currently no legal path to approving a drug for it, only for its individual downstream diseases.

The one that deserves more attention than it gets

Xenotransplantation is the quiet success story of this survey. Genetically modified pig kidneys and hearts have been transplanted into humans; kidney programmes have cleared phase 1/2 and are enrolling. It does not slow aging by a single day — it is a pure organ-supply solution — but organ failure is a major terminal pathway, and this is the nearest-term technology in the entire frontier landscape with the capacity to change outcomes at scale.

Where the highest-value missing tools are

The most useful thing a funder could do in several of these categories is not a therapy but a reagent. For crosslink breaking, the field has been stalled for two decades partly because there is no synthetic glucosepane standard and no glucosepane-specific antibody or assay in wide circulation — you cannot optimise a breaker you cannot measure. Tool-building is unglamorous, cheap relative to trials, and repeatedly the actual rate-limiting step.
