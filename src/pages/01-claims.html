Executive summary

The argument in one page

Six steps. Each is either arithmetic you can check yourself, or an empirical claim with a source. If step 2 is right, most of the field's stated goals are unreachable by the methods currently being used.

  1. Human mortality is exponential in age. From roughly 30 to 95, the annual chance of death doubles every ~8 years — the Gompertz law. This single exponent governs everything that follows.
  2. Any intervention that multiplies hazard by a constant is exactly equivalent to shifting age backwards by a fixed amount — and that amount is ln(k)/α, i.e. one mortality-rate doubling time per halving. Benefit is logarithmic in effort. We call this the Logarithmic Trap.
  3. This explains, from first principles, why curing entire disease categories buys so little. Eliminating all cancer or all cardiovascular disease yields single-digit years of life expectancy — not because oncology is weak, but because the Gompertz exponent compresses it. Our formula reproduces the published cause-deleted life-table results to within about half a year.
  4. The alternative — reducing α itself — is not ruled out by physics. The naked mole-rat's mortality hazard does not increase with age at all, across 3,299 animals and 30 years. α is a species-tunable parameter, not a constant of nature. Aging is not entropy in any sense that forbids intervening.1
  5. But the field cannot currently tell the two apart. Fitting the Gompertz intercept and slope from survival data is a badly-posed problem that yields a degenerate manifold of solutions; and aging clocks measure state, not rate, and none has been qualified as a surrogate endpoint by any regulator. Both of the field's main instruments are blind to the one parameter that matters.
  6. So we propose two fixes. A non-parametric quantile–quantile test that is identifiable where the Gompertz fit is not, and separates offset from rate in any lifespan experiment; and a within-subject rate readout — the resilience time-constant — that makes "is this drug slowing aging?" answerable in weeks instead of decades.
The central claim

Almost everything that works is an offset. Nothing demonstrated in humans is a rate change. The two are routinely reported with the same statistic — "median lifespan +X%" — which is precisely the statistic that cannot distinguish them. The field is not short of biology. It is short of a measurement that separates the two things it most needs to separate.
