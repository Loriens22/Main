The mathematics of mortality

Everything in this document rests on one empirical regularity, discovered by an actuary in 1825 and never seriously overturned: the risk of dying rises exponentially with age, and it does so at a remarkably conserved rate.

Let μ(t) be the hazard — the instantaneous probability per unit time of dying, given that you are alive at age t. Benjamin Gompertz observed that in adult humans this quantity is very nearly exponential. William Makeham later added a constant term to absorb causes of death that do not care how old you are: accidents, violence, most infectious disease.

Gompertz–Makeham law of mortality μ(t)  =  λ  +  A·eαt
μ(t) hazard at age t (per year)
λ Makeham term — age-independent risk
A Gompertz intercept — baseline vulnerability
α Gompertz slope — the rate of aging

The slope α is the quantity this entire document is about. It has a more intuitive form: the mortality rate doubling time, the number of years it takes for your risk of death to double.

Mortality rate doubling time MRDT  =  ln 2 / α   ≈   8 years in humans   ⟹   α ≈ 0.0866 yr−1

Eight years. That number is the exchange rate at which all medical progress is converted into lifespan, and in §3 we will show that it is a far harsher exchange rate than the field generally acknowledges.
