Errors and corrections

Named problems in named studies, with the specific fix in each case. This is offered in the spirit the field itself requires — these are almost all good scientists making structural mistakes that the incentives of the literature reward.

The striking thing about assembling this ledger is how few of the errors are about biology. Nearly all are about estimands, baselines and denominators — choosing a statistic that flatters the result, or omitting the number that would let a reader calibrate it.

Classification of the specific errors catalogued across all five domain reviews. Reporting and estimand choice dominates. Study or claimClass The flawThe correction
"36% increased survival" from senolyticsestimand The result was a 36% increase in remaining life in mice treated at 24–27 months — a survivorship-selected cohort. It is quoted almost universally as a 36% lifespan extension. Report (1) median total lifespan from birth in days, (2) median post-treatment survival in days, and (3) the RMST difference with 95% CI over a pre-specified horizon. RMST is the correct estimand: interpretable in time units, no proportional-hazards assumption, and immune to inflation by a late start age.
"+109% lifespan" from reprogrammingestimand Median remaining lifespan measured from 124 weeks against controls living ~129 weeks: ~109% of ~5 weeks is roughly +5 weeks absolute, about +4% of total lifespan — a ~25× gap to the headline. All authors company-affiliated; no independent replication. Percentage changes in remaining lifespan must never appear without the absolute baseline in the same sentence. Replicate as: AAV-OSK in UM-HET3, from 18 months, ≥60/arm/sex, two independent sites, pre-registered, with empty-capsid controls to exclude vector effects.
Senescent-cell clearance maximum-lifespan claimstatistics Maximum lifespan reached significance only with sexes pooled (p = 0.0295), after the sex-stratified analyses failed, and not at all in a second genetic background. With six comparisons in the family, that p-value survives no correction. Quantile regression at the 90th percentile, sex pre-specified as a stratification factor rather than a pooling convenience, background as a fixed effect, cage as a random effect in a Cox frailty model, Holm correction across the full family. Falsifiable prediction: the maximum-lifespan effect will not survive; the median effect probably will.
First-in-human senolytic studydesign n = 9, open-label, no control group — and it later required a corrigendum stating that re-analysis of the raw data changed some conclusions. It remains the load-bearing citation for "senolytics work in humans"; the corrigendum is essentially never cited. Anyone citing the paper must cite the corrigendum. Replace with: randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, n ≥ 60/arm, pre-registered, public raw data, paired biopsies read blind, multi-marker senescence score with myeloid exclusion — not p16 alone.
The p16-based senescence literature generallyconstruct validity p16INK4a and SA-β-gal are induced in macrophages as a reversible immune response and can appear in p53-null mice. Every clearance experiment ablates an unknown mixture of senescent cells and activated macrophages. Mandate multi-marker scoring, simultaneous myeloid-lineage exclusion at single-cell resolution, and inducible lineage-tracing controls that spare p16⁺ myeloid cells.
Reprogramming lifespan resultsmodel validity The headline +33% median came exclusively from progeroid mice with baselines measured in weeks. Rescuing a monogenic lamin defect is not evidence about normal aging, and short baselines inflate percentages. Cite the negative wild-type result rather than the progeroid one when making claims about normal aging. Definitive test: cyclic OSKM in UM-HET3, both sexes, ≥60/arm/sex, ≥2 sites, from 12 months, pre-registered, control lifespan reported in days.
Short-lived control cohorts, field-widebaseline A percentage lifespan gain is uninterpretable without the control's absolute lifespan. If controls are unusually short-lived, the "intervention" may be rescuing a husbandry or strain pathology rather than slowing aging. Require control median lifespan in days, per sex and per background, alongside every percentage — as journals require CONSORT items in clinical trials. Treat any percentage published without its baseline as uninterpretable.
"Metformin users outlive non-diabetics"confounding Observational comparison subject to immortal time bias and confounding by indication. The claim has driven enormous interest in a drug that then failed to extend lifespan alone in the ITP. Time-varying exposure modelling with a proper landmark or target-trial emulation design. The definitive answer requires the randomised trial, which has still not been run.
The alcohol "J-curve"confounding Abstainer bias: the reference group contains people who quit because they were ill. This produced decades of advice that moderate drinking is protective. Bias-adjusted meta-analysis plus Mendelian randomisation — both of which dissolve the protective effect. A worked example of reverse causation surviving in the literature for a generation.
"Evidence for a limit to human lifespan of ~115"statistics Segmented regression with a post-hoc breakpoint, using only the single oldest death per year and thus discarding most of the information. Five rebuttals followed in the same journal. Use the full distribution of extreme ages with extreme-value or Bayesian hierarchical methods; treat the breakpoint as a parameter with uncertainty rather than a choice; report sensitivity to it.
Supercentenarian records and Blue Zonesdata quality Records concentrate in regions with poor birth registration; birth certificates cut recorded counts 69–82%; birthdates heap on days divisible by five; regions reporting most 105+ report fewer 90+. Machine-checkable provenance standard: contemporaneous document required, pension-incentive independence demonstrated, automatic Whipple/Myers age-heaping test reported for every cohort before it enters a lifespan-limit dataset.
Epigenetic clocks as trial endpointssurrogacy Trained on chronological age — so their biological content lives in their residual; not qualified as a surrogate by any regulator; responsiveness is routinely mistaken for surrogacy; technical noise is rarely reported alongside effects. Train on time-to-death, not chronological age. Report ICC alongside every result and require effects to exceed the noise floor. Formally test Prentice criterion 4 before any clock is used as a decision-grade endpoint.
Gompertz slope–intercept fittingidentifiability Joint least-squares fitting of A and α is ill-posed; the parameters are collinear and the optimiser returns a degenerate manifold. Much of the Strehler–Mildvan literature rests on this artifact. Never fit A and α separately. Use the non-parametric tests of §4 — Q–Q decomposition, SD of age at death, coefficient of variation — none of which touch the degeneracy.
Resilience limit of 120–150 yearsextrapolation A linear fit extrapolated to its zero-crossing far outside the observed data range. Two routes to the same parameter — recovery-time ratio and inverse-variance — appear to disagree by 15–40% (§10). Report the critical age as a range with estimator sensitivity shown, and reconcile the two estimators explicitly. Where they disagree, the disagreement is the finding.
"Aging is thermodynamically inevitable"conceptual Organisms are open dissipative systems; the germline has persisted ~4 billion years without aging. The second law does not forbid indefinite somatic maintenance. Restate the argument where it actually bites — in information fidelity and the germline/soma asymmetry — rather than in energy or entropy generally.
Free-radical / oxidative damage theoryfalsified Antioxidant trials in humans range from null to harmful; several mouse models with elevated oxidative stress live normally or longer. Retire the strong form. It dominated the field for decades and left behind a supplement industry that outlived its evidence base.
Longevity escape velocityconceptual Coherent as arithmetic, but only if interventions are rate changes. Applied to offsets it fails immediately: each additional decade costs a further ~2.3-fold hazard reduction, permanently. State which parameter the argument assumes is moving. Escape velocity cannot be reached by stacking offsets — see §3.
